The Windstorm

A weekly dispatch from the eye of the storm

Week of September 7, 2026

Welcome to the Eye of the Windstorm.

This week the tools learned to run the computer themselves — and proved, again, that they were never the bottleneck. On Wednesday OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Astra, a model that doesn’t just answer you but operates your machine: opening spreadsheets, filling forms, clicking through the web on its own. The day before, Nvidia agreed to buy Hugging Face — the home of three million open models — for roughly thirteen billion dollars. The capability curve went vertical again. And the question that decides whether any of it matters inside your company is exactly the one it was last Monday: does anyone here know what to ask it to do?

1. The machine now drives

Greg Brockman said Astra can “zip through spreadsheets, fill out forms, and navigate across web pages often at superhuman speed.” It trained on more than a hundred thousand GPUs — the largest run in OpenAI’s history — and runs on the API for ten dollars a million words in, fifty out. Here is what I want you to sit with: the part everyone will photograph is the robot hand on the mouse. The part that actually decides winners is whether your team can describe a task cleanly enough that the machine can finish it. The tool got a thousand times more capable overnight. Your ability to delegate to it did not. That gap — not the model — is your bottleneck.

2. Cisco flipped the switch for 90,000 people

On Monday Cisco gave every one of its roughly ninety thousand employees a personal AI agent — “MyAgent” — that knows their role, their team, their recent work, and does the routine parts of the job for them. Not a pilot. Not a lab. The whole company, at once. This is the electricity story in real time: buying the power was never the hard part; wiring every room and teaching every person to flip the switch is. What took the last industrial revolution forty years, Cisco is trying to do in a quarter. Most companies are still standing at the fuse box.

3. The electricity got cheaper — again

Anthropic released Fable 5.1 with the same performance as June’s model at about twenty-five percent less compute, and cut the price of re-reading cached context from a dollar per million tokens to a quarter — a seventy-five percent drop — while keeping the million-token window that lets it hold a whole codebase or contract at once. Translation for the rest of us: the high-volume, boring, valuable work — the support inbox, the document pipeline, the reconciliation — just got materially cheaper to hand off. The meter is falling. What are you still doing by hand out of habit?

The Eye

Here is the thing nobody shouting this week will tell you: we have all quietly become child monarchs. You now sit at the head of a table of advisors, each of whom knows a thousand times what you do about their craft — and the crown does not require you to be the smartest person in the room. It requires you to ask the right question, listen to the answer, and decide. That is the whole job now. The Socratic method was never a teaching trick; it was a description of power. The person who asks the sharpest question conducts the room.

And here is why it is hard, and why nobody is a virtuoso yet: the scarce skill of this decade isn’t generating — the machines generate infinitely. It is winnowing. Knowing which of the ten answers is the right one, which draft to kill, which task is even worth handing over. We are all standing in a wind of infinite output, and the calm at the center — the eye — is simply the discipline of judgment. That can’t be bought at ten dollars a million tokens. It has to be practiced. So practice it.

Do this with your agent this week

Pick the one task you dread most on Monday morning — the report, the reconciliation, the inbox triage. Then don’t ask the agent to do it. Ask it this instead: “What questions should I answer for you before you could do this as well as I do?” Answer them. You will learn more about your own job, and about conducting, in fifteen minutes than in a month of reading hype. The delegation is the skill. Start there.
