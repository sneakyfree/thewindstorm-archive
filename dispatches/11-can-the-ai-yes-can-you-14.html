The Windstorm

A weekly dispatch from the eye of the storm

Week of September 14, 2026

Welcome to the Eye of the Windstorm.

Gartner's standing call is the number to hold against your own quarter: 40% of enterprise apps carrying a task-specific AI agent by the end of this year, up from under 5% in 2025. Forty months of change squeezed into ten. And yet a separate count cited this week found only 31% of companies have even a single agent actually running in production. Read those two numbers side by side and you have the whole story of this decade. The capability is arriving faster than anyone can absorb it. The tools are the easy part. The gap is human, not technical.

1. The agents got names

Salesforce unveiled seven named agents this week — Casey for support, Hunter for outbound sales, Marshall for the supply chain, Piper for pipeline, and three more. Some have started calling this class of software “persistent” agents: they do not wait for you to open a session, they just keep working in the background. Here is the part nobody is saying out loud. Giving them names was the smart move, because a tool you operate is a different thing from a colleague you delegate to. And knowing how to delegate to something that understands a thousand times what you do about supply-chain logistics — that skill is not in anybody’s job description yet.

2. OpenAI wants you to just ask

On September 10, OpenAI put a Data agent inside ChatGPT Work. Point it at Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks, or your dashboards in Tableau and Power BI, and instead of writing a query you ask a question. “Why did sales slow last month?” “Where is spending creeping up?” It investigates, then builds you a dashboard you can share. This is the Socratic method turned into a product. The person who wins here is not the one who knows SQL. It is the one who knows which question to ask — and asks it plainly.

3. Who is actually doing this

Under the headline sits a split worth staring at. By one widely shared breakdown, banking and insurance are running agents in production at close to half. Government and healthcare sit under a fifth. Same tools, offered to everyone, at the same price. A gap of two or three times that has nothing to do with the technology and everything to do with which industries decided to wire the building and teach their people to flip the switch. That gap is a choice. And it compounds every single week you leave it open.

The Eye

Here is what this week is actually about, underneath the product launches. For three years we kept asking “can the AI do it?” and the answer kept coming back yes. This week the question quietly changed. Now it is “can we?” Forty percent of business software carrying an agent by Christmas is already decided — it is out of your hands, arriving whether you are ready or not. What is not decided is whether the agent sitting inside your company ever does anything worth doing. That depends entirely on the people around it, and the people are the slow part.

Electricity took forty years to change the factory — not because the power was not there, but because someone had to rewire the building and teach every worker to flip a switch they had never seen. We are compressing that forty into four, which means the wiring and the teaching are both happening right now, in real time, mostly by people making it up as they go. You are a child monarch at a table of advisors who each know a thousand times what you do, and the whole job is knowing which of their thousand offers is the one worth acting on. Nobody is a virtuoso at that winnowing yet. That is oddly good news: the gap is not behind you. You can still close it. Most will not.

Do this with your agent this week

Pick one number you check every Monday — pipeline, cash, churn, whatever you actually watch. This week, instead of pulling the report yourself, hand your agent the data and ask it one open question: “What changed here that I would not have noticed?” Do not tell it what to look for. The whole point is to practice asking instead of instructing. Do it three Mondays running and watch how much sharper your questions get.
